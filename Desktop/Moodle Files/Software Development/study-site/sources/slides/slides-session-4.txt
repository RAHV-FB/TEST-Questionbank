===== [slides-session-4.pdf] page 9 =====
The Product Backlog
Ordered. Emergent. Never finished.
Items are outcomes, not tasks: "a returning user can log in with an email link"
Ordered by the Product Owner, top = next
Refined continuously, in detail only near the top
Estimating item 40 precisely is waste: it will change before it is built.

===== [slides-session-4.pdf] page 10 =====
Definition of Done
The quality bar every Increment meets — decided once, applied to everything.
Compiles · tested · reviewed · documented · deployable
Things like not "done except for tests" are not admissible.
An item that misses the Definition of Done is not done, and does not ship.

===== [slides-session-4.pdf] page 12 =====
What the ceremonies are not
Daily Scrum is not a status report to a manager. Fifteen minutes, for the Developers. PO
can join but not expected to participate. Each developer should take 1min to state:
Their latest work
What they intend on working next
Whether they have any blockers
Review is not a demo for applause. Stakeholders come to give feedback and change the
backlog
Retrospective is not blame. It's about discussing as a team what worked and what did
not, and what changes can be introduced accordingly
The PO retains the power to cancel the Sprint; this almost never happens

Pillars and values
Pillars: transparency · inspection · adaptation.
Values: commitment · focus · openness · respect · courage.
Inspection without transparency inspects a fiction. Adaptation without courage produces the
same Sprint, twelve times.

===== [slides-session-4.pdf] page 14 =====
What Scrum does not give you
No requirements document
No promise about what is in version 1.0, a year out
No architecture, no testing strategy, no engineering practices at all
Scrum organises the work; it does not do it.
Just like there is a tendency to imitate Agile development by mocking the Scrum ceremonies,
there is an equally dangerous tendency to believe that following the ceremonies around
Scrum is enough to get real work done.

===== [slides-session-4.pdf] page 22 =====
Why src/
Without src/ , import expense_tracker finds the folder you are standing in — whether or
not the package is installed.
With src/ , the import only works if the project is installed. The tests then exercise what a
user would get, not what happens to be lying next to them.
We get a separation between the deployable code in src/ and the tests in tests/ , which
are typically not included in the build.
Alberto Cámara | Software Development for Business | Session 4

===== [slides-session-4.pdf] page 24 =====
uv.lock
pyproject.toml says typer>=0.12 . The lockfile says exactly which typer, and which
of its dependencies, down to the hash.
Committed. CI runs uv sync --locked and fails if the two disagree.
"Works on my machine" becomes a problem that can usually be pinpointed to a lockfile that
was never committed.
